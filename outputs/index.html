Technical analysis for SaaS operating metrics

SaaS Signals Board

A Python data project that adapts technical-analysis logic to SaaS operating metrics. It turns subscription data into period-based growth, churn and revenue-quality signals, then translates those signals into plain business readings. The final board combines range breaks, momentum, persistence, baseline-shift detection and known-event annotations so teams can investigate unusual movements before they become larger revenue, retention or operating problems.

Why this project exists

SaaS dashboards usually show what happened: MRR, ARR, churn, ARPU, LTV, CAC, MAU, customer growth, retention, expansion revenue or NRR. Those views are useful, but often descriptive. SaaS Signals Board adds a second layer: is the movement normal for this metric, is it deteriorating or improving, is it isolated or persistent, and is the baseline itself changing? For fast-growing SaaS companies, the board applies range signals mainly to rates and changes rather than raw revenue levels, because absolute MRR can rise quickly without mean-reverting.

From dashboard number to business story

The goal is not to predict the future. The goal is to detect unusual movements early enough to investigate the cause, react faster and prevent small operating signals from becoming larger financial or business problems. The Metric Signal Summary is written as an executive read: what is happening, why it matters, whether it is fresh or persistent, and what type of driver deserves attention.

How the signals work

The board is built around four complementary questions. Is the metric outside its normal operating range? Is it improving or deteriorating versus the previous period and versus the same period last year? Is the abnormal movement isolated or repeating? Has the baseline itself moved to a new level? A period can be a day, week, month or quarter depending on the frequency selected in the board.

Bollinger Bands

Range signal. The metric is compared with its rolling average and expected volatility. A signal appears when it moves outside the range that would normally be expected from its own history.

Centralrolling average over N periods
Uppercentral band + k x rolling standard deviation
Lowercentral band - k x rolling standard deviation
Simple logic The average says where normal is centered. The standard deviation says how much the metric usually moves. k decides how much margin is allowed before a movement becomes unusual.

Momentum

Directional change. The metric is compared with the previous period and the same period last year, so the board can separate short-term movement from seasonal context.

Previouscurrent value - previous period value
Seasonalcurrent value - same period last year value
Simple logic Previous-period momentum shows the latest direction. Same-period momentum checks whether the metric is also better or worse than the comparable season last year.

Persistence

Measures whether a range break is isolated or repeating over consecutive periods. A single break may be noise; repeated breaks suggest the movement is becoming a pattern.

Simple logic Persistence is the duration layer. It tells whether the signal is fresh, already repeating, or persistent enough to treat as an operating issue.

Baseline Shift

Structural-change signal. The metric's recent average is compared with its previous baseline to detect whether the normal level itself may have changed.

Recentaverage of the last 3 periods
Baselineaverage of the previous 12 periods
Shiftrecent average - previous baseline
Simple logic Range Signal detects abnormal points. Baseline Shift detects whether the normal level itself may be changing. The area between the two lines is shaded green when the shift is favorable and red when it is unfavorable.
Rolling window Uses one full year whenever possible: 365 periods for daily data, 52 for weekly, 12 for monthly and 4 for quarterly. This reduces seasonal noise.
Sensitivity margin k Lower k creates tighter bands and earlier signals. Higher k creates wider bands and fewer, stronger signals.
Scenarios Early Signal, Normal and Flexible compare different tolerance settings. The user can rename them, change k values or add more scenarios.

Simple example with invented numbers

Imagine monthly MRR growth. The last 12 months have an average growth rate of 8.0% and a standard deviation of 2.0%. The user selected k=1.25. This is how the board turns numbers into signals.

Range signal Bollinger Bands

Central band = 8.0%. Upper band = 10.5%. Lower band = 5.5%. If current MRR growth is 4.0%, it is below the normal range, so the board reads it as unusually weak growth.

Early signal Momentum

Previous-period momentum = -3.0%. Same-period momentum = -5.0% versus last year. The story is not just that growth is low; it is weaker than both the previous month and the comparable season.

Duration Persistence

If MRR growth was also outside the range in the previous two months, the signal has lasted 3 consecutive periods. The board treats that as a pattern that deserves attention, not just noise.

Structural change Baseline Shift

If the last 3 months average 4.0% growth while the previous 12 months averaged 8.0%, the shift is -4.0%. The chart shades that gap red because the recent baseline is materially worse.

Metrics included in the MVP

The board focuses on five SaaS KPIs that can be calculated directly from the subscription and churn data. Each metric has its own business interpretation in the Metric Signal Summary, because a negative movement means something different for growth than it does for churn.

Revenue growth

MRR Growth

Period-over-period growth rate in monthly recurring revenue. The summary distinguishes between slower growth and actual MRR contraction, because both are risks but they do not tell the same story.

MRR growth = current MRR / previous MRR - 1
Revenue quality

ARPU Growth

Period-over-period growth rate in average revenue per active customer. The summary reads this as a signal about pricing, plan mix, expansion revenue or customer quality.

ARPU growth = current ARPU / previous ARPU - 1
Customer growth

Customer Growth

Period-over-period growth rate in active customers. The summary connects unusual movements to acquisition speed, retention pressure or changes in the active customer base.

Customer growth = current active customers / previous active customers - 1
Retention

Customer churn

Share of starting customers that churned during the period. For this metric, lower is better, so the same mathematical movement is interpreted in the opposite business direction from growth metrics.

Customer churn = churned customers / starting customers
Revenue retention

Revenue churn

Share of starting MRR lost during the period. The summary reads this as recurring revenue leakage, which matters directly for net revenue retention and revenue quality.

Revenue churn = churned MRR / starting MRR

These five KPIs keep the MVP focused on metrics that are important, interpretable and directly supported by the Kaggle subscription and churn data. Raw MRR and active customer counts are still useful context, but in a high-growth SaaS they can create noisy range signals because they may trend upward rather than oscillate around a stable mean. LTV is kept out of the signal board because monthly churn can make it jump sharply; the downloadable CSV keeps raw LTV and a smoothed LTV using 6-period average churn. CAC is not estimated because the dataset does not provide acquisition or marketing spend.

Context and interpretation

A signal is not automatically a problem. Some businesses have normal seasonal patterns: a June spike, a summer slowdown, a renewal month or a post-campaign decline. The board keeps that context visible with repeated x-axis labels across charts, same-period momentum, persistence reads and business-language summaries that explain what each movement may mean.

Known business events

The board lets the user annotate events such as price changes, large enterprise deals or campaign launches. An event can be added as a visual note or used to rebase the rolling bands from that month. This mirrors the analyst logic of saying: this period should not be compared with the old baseline because the business changed here.

Final readout

The Metric Signal Summary is designed to tell a story rather than repeat the chart. It explains whether growth is merely slower or actually contracting, whether churn is unusually high or favorably low, whether momentum is short-term or seasonal, whether the signal is fresh or persistent, and whether the recent baseline has moved to a new level.
